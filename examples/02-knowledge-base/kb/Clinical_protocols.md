# Emergency Medical Services (EMS) Clinical Reference Protocol

This document compiles the foundational assessment protocols, scales, and diagnostic mnemonics used by Emergency Medical Services (EMS) personnel during field triage, resuscitation, and primary stabilization.

---

## 1. ABCDE Protocol (Primary Assessment)
The **ABCDE** protocol forms the foundational workflow for the rapid, systematic identification and mitigation of immediate life-threatening injuries or conditions during the primary survey.

| Step | Assessment Category | Key Clinical Interventions & Checks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A** | **Airway** (with restriction of cervical spine motion) | • Check for patency (snoring, gurgling, stridor).<br>• Clear obstructions (suctioning, forceps).<br>• Open airway via jaw-thrust or head-tilt/chin-lift.<br>• Insert basic/advanced airways (OPA, NPA, supraglottic airway, ETI). |
| **B** | **Breathing** (Ventilation & Oxygenation) | • Assess rate, depth, and effort of respiration.<br>• Inspect chest expansion and look for deformities (flail chest).<br>• Auscultate breath sounds bilaterally.<br>• Provide supplemental $O_2$ or bag-valve-mask (BVM) ventilation.<br>• Decompress tension pneumothorax if indicated. |
| **C** | **Circulation** (Perfusion & Hemorrhage) | • Assess central and peripheral pulses (rate, quality).<br>• Evaluate skin color, temperature, and capillary refill.<br>• Control major external exsanguination (tourniquet, direct pressure).<br>• Establish IV/IO access and initiate appropriate fluid resuscitation. |
| **D** | **Disability** (Neurological Status) | • Evaluate level of consciousness using **AVPU** (Alert, Verbal, Pain, Unresponsive) or **GCS**.<br>• Check pupillary size, equality, and reactivity.<br>• Assess gross motor and sensory function in all extremities.<br>• Perform blood glucose analysis for altered mental status. |
| **E** | **Exposure & Environment** | • Expose the patient fully to examine for hidden traumatic injuries.<br>• Prevent hypothermia using warm blankets, heated fluids, and climate control.<br>• Maintain patient modesty and dignity once evaluation is complete. |

## 2. OPQRST Protocol (Pain & Symptom Assessment)
The **OPQRST** mnemonic guides the clinician through an organized, chronological evaluation of a patient's primary complaint, focusing heavily on cardiovascular, respiratory, and acute pain presentations.

*   **Onset**
    *   *Question:* "What were you doing when the pain/symptom started?"
    *   *Context:* Sudden onset vs. gradual development (e.g., sudden onset can indicate acute ischemic events, embolism, or rupture).
*   **Provocation / Palliation**
    *   *Question:* "Does anything make the pain better or worse? Does moving, breathing, or pressing on it change it?"
    *   *Context:* Ischemic chest pain is typically non-reproducible by palpation, whereas musculoskeletal pain often changes with position or pressure.
*   **Quality**
    *   *Question:* "Can you describe the pain? Is it sharp, dull, crushing, tearing, squeezing, or burning?"
    *   *Context:* "Crushing" or "squeezing" commonly correlates with myocardial ischemia; "tearing" or "ripping" may point toward aortic dissection.
*   **Radiation / Region**
    *   *Question:* "Where is the pain located? Does it move or travel anywhere else (e.g., arm, jaw, back)?"
    *   *Context:* Cardiac ischemia frequently radiates to the left arm, neck, jaw, or epigastrium.
*   **Severity**
    *   *Question:* "On a scale of 0 to 10, with 0 being no pain and 10 being the worst pain imaginable, how would you rate it right now?"
    *   *Context:* Establishes a subjective baseline to track treatment efficacy (e.g., response to oxygen, nitroglycerin, or analgesics).
*   **Time**
    *   *Question:* "How long ago did this symptom begin? Has it been constant, or does it come and go?"
    *   *Context:* Establishes critical time windows for fibrinolytic therapies, PCI, or other time-sensitive protocols.

## 3. Stroke Scale Reference
Prehospital stroke screens identify possible large-vessel occlusions or acute ischemic stroke pathways, initiating early destination notification to Comprehensive or Primary Stroke Centers.

### Cincinnati Prehospital Stroke Scale (CPSS)
Any single abnormal finding yields a high probability of acute stroke.

1.  **Facial Droop**
    *   *Instruction:* Have the patient show their teeth or smile.
    *   *Normal:* Both sides of the face move equally.
    *   *Abnormal:* One side of the face does not move as well as the other side, causing asymmetry.
2.  **Arm Drift**
    *   *Instruction:* Have the patient close their eyes and extend both arms straight out in front of them with palms up for 10 seconds.
    *   *Normal:* Both arms move the same or do not move at all.
    *   *Abnormal:* One arm does not move, or one arm drifts down compared to the other.
3.  **Abnormal Speech**
    *   *Instruction:* Have the patient say, "You can't teach an old dog new tricks."
    *   *Normal:* The patient uses correct words with no slurring.
    *   *Abnormal:* The patient slurs words, uses inappropriate words, or is completely unable to speak.

## 4. Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS)
The **Glasgow Coma Scale** calculates an objective numerical score reflecting a patient's depth of consciousness based on three distinct behavioral response criteria.

### Scoring Breakdown

#### Eye Opening Response (E)
*   **4** = Spontaneous (opens eyes without stimulus)
*   **3** = To Speech (opens eyes when spoken to or commanded)
*   **2** = To Pain / Pressure (opens eyes only upon painful/pressure stimulus)
*   **1** = None (no eye opening under any circumstances)

#### Verbal Response (V)
*   **5** = Oriented (knows name, location, month/year)
*   **4** = Confused (conversational but disoriented to time/place)
*   **3** = Inappropriate Words (random, disjointed, or shouted words; no sustained conversation)
*   **2** = Incomprehensible Sounds (moaning, groaning; no recognizable words)
*   **1** = None (no vocalization)

#### Motor Response (M)
*   **6** = Obeys Commands (performs requested movements accurately)
*   **5** = Localizes Pain (moves hand toward a painful stimulus to remove it)
*   **4** = Normal Flexion / Withdrawal (pulls body part away from painful stimulus)
*   **3** = Abnormal Flexion (Decorticate posturing; arms flexed/adducted, fists clenched)
*   **2** = Extension (Decerebrate posturing; rigid extension of arms/legs, internal rotation)
*   **1** = None (flaccid, no movement)

### Clinical Interpretation & Summary
$$	ext{Total GCS Score} = E + V + M$$

*   **Maximum Score:** **15** (Fully awake, alert, and oriented)
*   **Minimum Score:** **3** (Deep coma or brain death)
*   **Classification Levels:**
    *   **Mild Brain Injury:** GCS 13 – 15
    *   **Moderate Brain Injury:** GCS 9 – 12
    *   **Severe Brain Injury (Coma):** GCS $\le$ 8 *(Clinical axiom: "GCS less than 8, intubate")*
